Method

How a product is born

They all follow the same process. The domain changes, the method does not.

  1. 01

    Experience

    I live the problem or see it up close. I don't start from an abstract business idea.

  2. 02

    Problem

    I separate the one-off annoyance from the structural problem: scattered information, a manual process or a regulation that is hard to turn into software.

  3. 03

    Specification

    I write the spec before the code: scope, domain, users and what "it works" means. That is where the most time is saved.

  4. 04

    Product

    End-to-end MVP: data model, API, frontend and deploy. No frameworks or infrastructure the problem doesn't ask for.

  5. 05

    Capability

    Every product leaves something reusable behind: a billing engine, a multi-tenant pattern, an audio pipeline.

About me

From building systems to enabling engineering teams

I started out writing backend code. Today my job is helping teams, products and platforms deliver value sustainably. Each stage widened the system I was responsible for.

  1. 01

    Developer

    Computer science fundamentals, Java, REST APIs, Spring, persistence and enterprise messaging. Quality became concrete: a failure could mean a transaction that never happened or an integration nobody could recover.

    ScopeCode, data and consistency.

  2. 02

    Senior and technical leader

    Banking, payments and fintech widened the problem: reconciliation, auditing, security, regulation, webhooks and third-party integrations. My responsibility grew to include standards, architecture decisions and reducing technical risk.

    ScopeOperable systems and technical decisions.

  3. 03

    Engineering Manager

    The focus shifted to cross-functional teams, architecture, platform, priorities, mentoring and communication with the business. Less code written directly; more decisions that enable sustained delivery.

    ScopeTeams, delivery and technical strategy.

Where I am heading

Next scope: executive leadership

I'm looking for roles where technology, product and business align. CTO: connecting architecture, security and platform with strategy. VP or Head of Engineering: scaling talent, delivery and team health. Head of Product: bringing together discovery, prioritization and technical feasibility. These are goals, not current titles.

How I work

Six things I learned by building

Technology starts with understanding the problem

The useful question isn't which stack to use, but what is happening to the person who will use it. The stack comes later, and it is almost always more boring than you'd like.

A useful product beats a complex demo

A page that actually sold the furniture of a whole house is worth more than an elegant architecture with nobody on the other side.

Architecture follows the business

Multi-tenancy, dual currency or an event ledger aren't technical decisions: they're business decisions written in code, and they're expensive if they arrive late.

AI amplifies judgment; it does not replace it

It speeds up researching, specifying and building. It's still on me to decide what gets built, review what it produces and own the result.

Building includes measuring, learning and iterating

That's why my projects ship with tests, auditable events and explicit state. If I can't tell what happened, I haven't finished building it.

Everyday problems hide opportunities

Moving house, caring for my dog or selling a car showed me broken processes nobody was looking at. The raw material is closer than it seems.
